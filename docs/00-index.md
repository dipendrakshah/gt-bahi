# 00 — How to read this repository

These documents freeze a product conversation that ran on **18 September 2026**. They are written so a founder, engineer, accountant, or future investor can reconstruct *why* the product is shaped this way without needing the original chat.

## Reading order

If you have one hour:

1. `01-original-scenario.md` — what was asked
2. `02-problem-and-thesis.md` — what we decided
3. `06-icp-and-positioning.md` — who it is for
4. `09-non-goals.md` — what we refuse
5. `16-roadmap.md` — what ships when
6. `15-unit-economics.md` — whether it can pay rent

If you have a day, read everything in numeric order.

## How the conversation narrowed

The user started with a wide map:

> What are the best business solutions in the Americas IT world? Shopify has a domain, QuickBooks has some value — what else and what are they solving for?

Then asked how Nepal addresses the same layers, then how new front doors (Blanxer, Zalient) copy Shopify, then whether to build:

> accounting system + API + MCP + storefront + social media chat agents

and what business model and break-even year look like.

The honest conclusion, preserved here:

- The *domain* (order → money → stock → tax) is still enterable in Nepal.
- The *bundle as five products* is not enterable for a new team in year one.
- MCP is a distribution interface, not a company.
- Meta will own generic chat. Tally/Tigg will own generic books. Blanxer will own ads-to-courier.
- The remaining company is the **system of record in the middle**.

## Conventions in these docs

- Currency is **NPR** unless marked otherwise.
- Fiscal year means Nepal FY **Shrawan–Ashadh** (Bikram Sambat) unless marked AD.
- “Working name” = Bahi. Rename is allowed; the thesis is not.
- Claims about competitor scale are tagged as **company-claimed** when they come from vendor sites.
- Dates in research snapshots are as of mid-September 2026.

## What “exhaustive” means here

These files capture:

- The original questions and the chain of analysis
- Americas reference stack (so the analogy does not get lost)
- Nepal layer-by-layer maturity
- Front-door competitors in detail
- A narrowed product with objects, workflows, API, MCP tools
- Compliance constraints that kill global tools
- Pricing, burn, break-even scenarios
- A 36-month roadmap with kill criteria
- Open questions that must not be silently assumed

They do **not** contain production code, IRD certification paperwork, or a legal opinion. Compliance sections are product requirements, not advice from a licensed auditor.
